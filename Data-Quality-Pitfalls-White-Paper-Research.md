# Research: Data Quality Pitfalls — Failure Modes and Prevention Framework

Compiled source list backing the white paper *Data Quality Pitfalls: From Recurring Failure Modes to a Cross-Industry Prevention Framework*. Organized by taxonomy category (Section 2 of the white paper) rather than by industry, since the taxonomy is this paper's primary organizing axis — industry vignettes (Section 4) are cross-cutting evidence, not the spine.

**Status: first sourcing pass complete.** Every entry below was checked this session against a primary or independently-corroborated source (WebSearch/WebFetch), not pulled from memory. `[VERIFIED]` means a primary source was fetched and confirmed directly, or confirmed via ISO's/the publisher's own catalog listing. `[VERIFIED — fetch blocked]` means the title/scope was confirmed via a search result or catalog listing but the full document returned a 403 to automated fetch (retry manually before final citation). `[COULD NOT FULLY VERIFY]` means only secondary/repeated citations were found, no primary source — treat as an industry-repeated estimate, not confirmed fact, and consider dropping if a better source isn't found. One candidate (Zillow Offers) was actively **rejected** after verification — see Section 4 — rather than kept on the strength of being a well-known story.

## 1. Structural Pitfalls (Section 2.1)

- **[VERIFIED] Sanderson, C. (2022).** *The Rise of Data Contracts — And Why Your Data Pipelines Don't Scale.* dataproducts.substack.com, Aug 22, 2022. Frames production databases as "non-consensual APIs" between engineering and data teams: undocumented schema changes cascade into broken downstream pipelines, eroded trust, and technical debt (his "GIGO Cycle"). Strong, well-known practitioner anchor for why schema drift specifically breaks things. (Full citation and the governance-movement angle on the same piece are in Section 6 — this is the same source used for two different points in the paper.) Link: https://dataproducts.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-data-contracts
- **[CANDIDATE — NOT FULLY VERIFIED] "Schema Versioning in Databases: A Literature Review."** Found on ResearchGate (2024/2025); presents a taxonomy of schema-change operations (add/delete/rename/type-change/constraint-change) across relational, XML, and NoSQL models. Full text is behind ResearchGate's request-access wall — author names, exact venue, and year are unconfirmed. Do not cite as peer-reviewed until verified directly. Link: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385043030_Schema_Versioning_in_Databases_A_Literature_Review

## 2. Semantic Pitfalls (Section 2.2)

- **[VERIFIED — vendor blog, cite as illustration not authority] dbt Labs.** *How the dbt Semantic Layer Works.* Documents "metric drift" concretely: the same named metric (e.g., "monthly active users," "revenue") is independently redefined in a BI tool, a notebook, and a dashboard, producing different "correct" numbers because the underlying SQL logic differs (e.g., Sales counts pending invoices, Finance doesn't). Best available concrete illustration for this section. It's a vendor blog — flag per this paper's sourcing-tier convention — but the phenomenon it names is a widely corroborated, non-vendor-specific problem, not a claim unique to dbt's product. Link: https://www.getdbt.com/blog/how-the-dbt-semantic-layer-works

## 3. Identity & Duplication Pitfalls (Section 2.3)

- **[VERIFIED — lead citation, peer-reviewed] BMJ Quality & Safety (2025).** "Double trouble: a propensity-matched cohort study evaluating the associations between duplicate medical records and patient outcomes." DOI: 10.1136/bmjqs-2025-019112. Cohort of 6,086 patients (July 2022–June 2023) drawn from 103,190 records reviewed; duplicate-record prevalence estimated at 5–10%. Patients with duplicate records had inpatient death at 11% vs. 2.5% (~5x adjusted risk), ICU admission 46% vs. 19% (~3.5x adjusted), and 32% longer length of stay after adjustment. This is a materially stronger citation than a vendor-blog duplicate-rate stat: peer-reviewed, a real DOI, and it frames duplication as a patient-safety/data-quality outcome rather than an identity-resolution product pitch — use this as the lead source for Section 2.3, not a supporting one. Secondary writeup: https://bmjgroup.com/duplicate-medical-records-linked-to-5-fold-heightened-risk-of-inpatient-death/ (cite the DOI as primary).
- **[COULD NOT FULLY VERIFY] AHIMA (~10%) / Black Book Research (~15–18%) duplicate-rate figures.** Widely repeated across vendor blogs (Verato, LeadSquared, Landbase) but no primary AHIMA report or original Black Book survey could be located to confirm exact figures or methodology. Treat as industry-repeated estimates only if used at all — the BMJ study above is the stronger citation and may make these unnecessary.

## 4. Statistical & Distributional Pitfalls, incl. ML (Section 2.4)

- **[VERIFIED] Sambasivan, N., Kapania, S., Highfill, H., Akrong, D., Paritosh, P., & Aroyo, L.M. (2021).** *"Everyone wants to do the model work, not the data work": Data Cascades in High-Stakes AI.* CHI '21, May 2021, Yokohama, Japan. DOI: 10.1145/3411764.3445518. Based on interviews with 53 AI practitioners across India, East/West Africa, and the US. Findings: 92% of practitioners reported experiencing one or more "data cascades" (compounding downstream failures triggered by an upstream data issue); 45.3% reported experiencing multiple cascades. Central argument: data work is structurally undervalued relative to model work, which is the root cause. Note: figures corroborated via independent secondary summaries (Google Research's own pub page, Gradient Flow, TAUS); ACM's full-text page returned 403 to automated fetch — do a primary-PDF spot check before final citation. Link: https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445518
- **[VERIFIED] Sculley, D., Holt, G., Golovin, D., Davydov, E., Phillips, T., Ebner, D., Chaudhary, V., Young, M., Crespo, J-F., & Dennison, D. (2015).** *Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems.* NeurIPS 2015. Names ML-specific data risk factors directly relevant to this section: entanglement ("CACE: Changing Anything Changes Everything" — changing one input feature changes the behavior of others), hidden feedback loops, undeclared consumers, and data dependencies that are as costly as code dependencies but far harder to detect. Strong second peer-reviewed anchor alongside Sambasivan et al. Link: https://papers.nips.cc/paper/5656-hidden-technical-debt-in-machine-learning-systems
- **[VERIFIED — strong, on-the-record case study] Unity Technologies (2022).** Q1 2022 earnings call, May 11, 2022. CEO John Riccitiello, verbatim: *"We lost the value of a portion of our data, training data due in part to us ingesting bad data from a large customer."* Estimated business impact, also verbatim: *"approximately $110 million in 2022 with no carryover impact to 2023"* (~60% Q2 / 30% Q3 / 10% Q4). Affected product: Audience Pinpointer, an ML-based ad-targeting/monetization tool. This is a clean, company-attributed, on-the-record data-quality-caused ML failure — a materially stronger source than a typical vendor-blog restatement of a similar claim. Primary source (transcript): https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2022/05/11/unity-software-inc-u-q1-2022-earnings-call-transcr/
- **[REJECTED — do not use] Zillow Offers shutdown (Nov 2021).** Initially proposed as a candidate case study; checked against the AI Incident Database, Zillow CEO Rich Barton's own public statement, and a Stanford GSB analysis. All three attribute the failure to forecasting unpredictability and strategic overreach (entering non-standardized housing markets, adverse selection, a speed-vs-accuracy tradeoff) — not to stale or low-quality input data. The Zestimate model reportedly had ample data available; Barton's own framing is a forecasting-model failure, not a data-quality failure. Using this example would be exactly the kind of sourcing overreach this paper's CLAUDE.md commits to avoiding. Figures for reference only, not for citation here: $304.4M Q3 2021 inventory write-down, up to $569M total expected writedowns, 25% workforce reduction.
- **See also Section 8 (Healthcare vignette):** de Andrade et al. (2026), *Critical Care*, documents a sepsis-detection ML model's AUC dropping from 0.76–0.83 internally to 0.63 externally due to EHR data-quality degradation — a second concrete ML/statistical-drift example, cross-referenced there rather than duplicated here.

## 5. Temporal Pitfalls (Section 2.5)

- **[VERIFIED] Uber Engineering Blog (Dec 11, 2025).** By Xinli Shang, Jing Li, Peter Huang, Jack Song, Jing Zhao. *"From Batch to Streaming: Accelerating Data Freshness in Uber's Data Lake."* States directly: "without faster downstream transformation and access, data remains stale at the point of decision," with business impact spanning experimentation, risk detection, personalization, and operational analytics. Their re-architecture (batch → streaming via Flink) cut latency from hours to minutes and reduced compute 25%. Strong primary-source anchor for the cost of staleness in real-time decisioning. Retry with a different fetch method before citing. Link: https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/visualizing-data-timeliness-at-airbnb-ee638fdf4710

## 9. Validation Checkpoints & Verification Depth (Section 3.2)

Sources anchoring the framework's lifecycle-checkpoint and verification-depth model (when validation happens, and how deep it goes — syntactic vs. internal-consistency vs. external ground-truth).

- **[VERIFIED, peer-reviewed] Schelter, S., Lange, D., Schmidt, P., Celikel, M., Grafberger, A., & Biessmann, F. (2018).** *Automating Large-Scale Data Quality Verification.* Proceedings of the VLDB Endowment, Vol. 11, No. 12 (VLDB 2018). Introduces a declarative system for defining data-quality constraints as "unit tests for data," executed as Apache Spark aggregation queries at scale, with ML-based constraint suggestion and anomaly detection on the resulting metrics. This is the paper behind Deequ, Amazon's open-source Spark data-quality library — directly on point for pre/in-flight/post validation implemented in a batch/Spark context, and a peer-reviewed anchor stronger than a vendor blog (e.g., Great Expectations, Soda) for this part of the framework. Link: https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol11/p1781-schelter.pdf
- **[VERIFIED] NIST Special Publication 800-63A.** *Digital Identity Guidelines — Enrollment and Identity Proofing.* Defines Identity Assurance Levels (IAL) as graduated confidence tiers for identity verification against authoritative sources, not a binary pass/fail: IAL1 (core attributes validated against authoritative sources, some assurance the applicant is associated with the identity), IAL2 (additional evidence and more rigorous validation, confirms the applicant genuinely owns the presented evidence), IAL3 (in-person/biometric verification by a trained representative, defends against sophisticated attacks). This is the standards-body anchor for confidence-scored external verification generally — the paper's phone/address examples are instances of the same graduated-confidence pattern this document formalizes for identity attributes. Link: https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63a.html
- **[VERIFIED] USPS, administered via the Addressing and Geospatial Technology (AGT) office.** *CASS™ (Coding Accuracy Support System) certification.* A software certification program evaluating address-matching accuracy against ZIP+4, delivery-point coding, carrier-route codes, and supporting tools (DPV®, DSF2®, LACSLink®, SuiteLink®); certification requires a minimum 98.5% match score across several of these. A concrete, named example of an authoritative third-party standard specifically for the address-verification case raised in this section — real infrastructure already used industry-wide for exactly this kind of external ground-truth check. Link: https://postalpro.usps.com/certifications/cass
- **[VERIFIED, primary paywalled] Fellegi, I.P. & Sunter, A.B. (1969).** *A Theory for Record Linkage.* Journal of the American Statistical Association, 64(328), 1183–1210. Foundational statistical model for record linkage; defines the three-way decision rule referenced in Section 3.2.2 (a match, a non-match, and a "possible match" in between requiring manual/clerical review) — the same graded-decision pattern this paper generalizes to confidence-scored external verification. Direct publisher access (Taylor & Francis) returned 403 to automated fetch; exact title/journal/volume/pages corroborated via independent secondary citation (Wikipedia's Record Linkage article, which quotes the three-way decision rule directly). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1969.10501049
- **[NOT YET RESEARCHED] Phone-to-owner verification standard.** No standards-body or peer-reviewed anchor found yet for the phone/customer-ownership example (analogous to CASS for address or IAL for identity) — carrier lookup / number-portability database checks (e.g., NANC's NPAC) are the likely real-world mechanism but haven't been verified. Flag as a gap; either research further or keep the phone example illustrative rather than citation-backed.
